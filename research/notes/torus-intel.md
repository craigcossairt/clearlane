

######## torus-intel
> Internal research on Torus's own business, written before the build to choose a concept. It is not a Clearlane claim: Clearlane's value is to Rocky Mountain Power's system, not Torus revenue.

## SUMMARY
Torus is a hardware company whose stations work as software-dispatched flexible load. The proven use is utility demand response on Rocky Mountain Power's system. Torus Stations (Spin flywheel, Pulse LFP battery, Lasso software, Shield cyber, Overwatch 24/7 monitoring) sit behind the meter at C&I sites under Rocky Mountain Power's Wattsmart Battery program. The host pays a monthly subscription with no upfront capital, and PacifiCorp dispatches the fleet as a virtual power plant: 359 events in 2025, a 70 MW MOU that grew to 500 MW, recognition from PLMA and Fast Company for managing fast-moving loads. Their pitch to utilities is congestion relief, non-wires alternatives and frequency/voltage support. Their CTO/CISO publicly describes grid congestion as a freeway at 5pm on a Friday, which is almost exactly Option 1's framing.

The data center / AI bet is real but less proven. The $200M Magnetar raise (Sept 2025) was framed around AI load. There is one named on-site deployment: Creekstone's Delta, Utah AI campus (Dec 2025, load smoothing). Their pages pitched GPU load smoothing, flywheel UPS, speed-to-power, and Texas SB6 curtailment compliance. Notable: between 2026-08-04 and today those data center pages went 404, and the live homepage dropped "AI" and "hyperscalers" from its headline. October 2026 press boilerplate still mentions data centers and AI-driven load growth. Why is unknown.

Recommendation: Option 1 maps more directly to the value Torus stations deliver to Rocky Mountain Power's system, and to Torus's roadmap. Build a "grid traffic report" that forecasts congested and critical hours for the PacifiCorp territory (PACE) and plans what flexible loads and storage should do: pre-charge before rush hour, shave peaks, stay out of traffic. Make an AI data center the showcase flexible load. That brings in Option 2's "when should compute run" overlap without needing multi-region routing. Torus does not route compute between regions. No software, data or ML roles are open now, so the pitch should complement Lasso, not compete with it. Torus also has a strong national-security and grid-security identity (2025 Zero Gravity Summit presenting partner).
## IMPLICATIONS
- Pick Option 1 as the backbone. Torus's revenue and its 2025-2026 awards come from utility-dispatched flexible load at the point of demand: Wattsmart VPP, a 500 MW MOU, feeder congestion and non-wires alternatives. Its own executives already use the rush-hour/freeway metaphor.
- Fold in Option 2's 'when' half, not its 'where' half. Use an AI data center (Creekstone Delta is the real Utah example) as the showcase flexible load whose training jobs and Torus storage get scheduled around forecast grid rush hours. Skip multi-region compute routing; Torus does not play there.
- A concrete build: a 'tomorrow's grid traffic report' for the PacifiCorp East (PACE) balancing area. Inputs: hourly demand from EIA-930 plus Open-Meteo Salt Lake City temperature and solar. Output: a ranked list of congested and critical hours with a risk score. A planner then makes a dispatch plan for a fleet of simulated Torus Stations (pre-charge before peaks, keep reserve for unplanned frequency events, shave host demand charges) and for a flexible AI training load (shift or curtail). Show MW of peak shaved, demand-charge dollars saved and DR readiness. Label the simulated fleet clearly.
- Insight worth leading with: RMP's Torus events are mostly unplanned frequency response (less than 1% of hours, 19-second ramps), so not every event can be predicted. The value is predicting when the risk is high and making sure state of charge is ready then, while still earning peak-shaving value the rest of the time. That speaks to both the utility and the host, Torus's 'customer wins, utility wins' line.
- Position the build as a public-data layer Lasso could consume, or a utility-fleet view of which hosts to dispatch and when. Do not present it as a Lasso replacement. Lasso already claims AI forecasting from load, weather and grid signals.
- Use Torus vocabulary in the pitch: point of demand, flexible load, mesh energy infrastructure, feeder congestion, months not years, system critical hours, controlled dispatch. Mention Operation Gigawatt and Utah data center load growth for the Utah and Nucleus judges.
- Nucleus $10K overlap: the same build can score on Utah impact (PacifiCorp territory, Operation Gigawatt) and path to reality (Wattsmart is a live program). Hard-tech depth is weaker for a pure software build, so present the physics plainly: flywheel 10C versus LFP duration, and why that split matters for scheduling.
## RISKS
- Torus removed its data center and AI pages and dropped 'AI' and 'hyperscalers' from its homepage some time between 2026-08-04 and 2026-10-26. Reason unknown: a site restructure, a de-emphasis, or a pending launch. Leaning on 'AI data center' as the main story could miss a current Torus priority. Leaning only on utilities could under-serve the 'Energy x AI infrastructure' bounty title.
- Torus has no open software, data or ML roles. Judges may value insight and the demo more than a pitch that implies Torus needs a new software product.
- Real-time PacifiCorp DR event data and Torus fleet telemetry are not public. The dispatch and fleet side must be simulated and labeled as such per the hackathon rules.
- Several Torus numbers are company-reported marketing claims (99.9% uptime, 10C, 20x battery power, 25,000 cycles, 500 MW). The 500 MW is an MOU ceiling, not verified operating capacity. Do not present these as independently verified.
- Grid-congestion software competes with established DERMS, VPP and forecasting vendors. Torus may see a generic forecaster as commodity unless the build ties to Torus-specific physics (flywheel and battery split, state-of-charge reserve for frequency events).
