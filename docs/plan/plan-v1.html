The call

Build Option 1, “Rush hour for electrons,” as the spine. Option 2 overlaps only through its “when” half, so it rides along as one closing card: in Utah, compute should follow the sun in time, not in space.

Clearlane is a day planner for Torus-style stations on Utah’s grid. It fills up in the midday clear lane, stays off the road in the 6pm jam, and keeps Spin ready for Rocky Mountain Power’s calls, all inside an hourly operating envelope RMP can audit.

Why Option 1 leads

  • Torus earns on utility-dispatched storage: 359 RMP calls in 2025, up from 137 in 2024.
  • Its CTO already describes the grid as a freeway at 5pm on a Friday.
  • In June 2026 Torus asked Utah regulators to standardize operating envelopes and define System Critical Hours. Clearlane exports that artifact.
  • It won the concept tally at 8.15 against 7.03, 6.44 and 5.95, and the Torus-lens judge’s grafts are folded in.

Why Option 2 is only a card

  • About nine near-identical carbon-aware job routers already exist; a EuroSys 2024 study found their savings modest.
  • Torus does not route compute, and its data-center pages return 404 as of Oct 26.
  • In October, shifting a block of load in time saved 40-41%; shifting it to another node saved 8%. PACE was the cheapest node in 81% of hours.

The insight, in four lines

  • Oct 27: the five overnight hours from 1am average $38.71/MWh; 9am to 5pm averages $22.83. Solar drops from 2,056 MW at 4pm to 36 MW at 6pm and net load climbs 3,654 MW, so price jumps 2.6 times.
  • Cheap and clean line up. In October the priciest 10% of hours were 85.1% fossil; the cheapest 10% were 39.8%. At 70 MW the overnight schedule adds about 18.4 t of CO2 a day and the timetable avoids about 18.9 t (average emissions from EIA, not marginal).
  • The 359 calls in 2025 add up to about 33 hours, 0.38% of the year. Holding the reserve inside the battery instead of in Spin costs about 3.4% of value, or misses calls.
  • A price-only plan misses between 14% and 77% of 2025-pattern calls. Which end applies depends only on whether Torus’s event heatmap is in Mountain time or UTC, and the source does not say.

How it ties to Torus

Spin

Gets the job its published specs fit (10C, 25,000 cycles, 12-minute discharge and recharge): the reserve for RMP calls, sized to the enrolled kW at 0.1 kWh per kW.

Pulse

Runs the timetable at a wear price calibrated to its 7,500 cycles over 25 years, about 300 a year, with a cycle meter on screen. A fixed nightly cycle uses 365.

Lasso

Already forecasts load, dispatches in under 250 ms and routes between Spin and Pulse. Not advertised: a timetable keyed to EDAM day-ahead prices, and an exportable envelope. Both could enter through its API as one more grid signal.

Wattsmart and RMP

Torus is one of two approved commercial vendors (30 kW to 1.2 MW, IEEE 2030.5). 2025: 359 events, 19 s ramp against a 30 s target, 102% delivered against a 90-100% band.

The June 2026 ask

Torus asked Utah regulators to credit controlled and scheduled dispatch, to standardize utility-directed operating envelopes, and to define System Critical Hours. Clearlane is a working example with a backtest behind it.

Hosts

Demand charges are set by a single 15- or 30-minute interval. The host lane caps Pulse charging and Spin refills under the host’s 15-minute ceiling, so the grid plan never sets a new host peak.

Guardrails on stage: stations are “simulated Torus-style” with no logos; the overnight schedule is never called Torus practice; anything Torus does not publish is “not advertised,” never “missing.”

The engine

  • A 15-minute dynamic program over Pulse state of charge.
  • Spin reserved for RMP calls at the enrolled kW.
  • The host’s 15-minute demand ceiling as a hard constraint.
  • A wear price that lands Pulse near 300 cycles a year.
  • Three policies compared: the off-peak rule, a price-only timetable, and Clearlane.

What runs live on stage

  • The day’s solve, the crash and the re-plan, each timed with performance.now().
  • The envelope export and the time-zone toggle.
  • DATA_MODE=snapshot by default: nothing on stage needs the network.
  • The same engine code runs in the browser and in the node CLIs that pin the numbers.
